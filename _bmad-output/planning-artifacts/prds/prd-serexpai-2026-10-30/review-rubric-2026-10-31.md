# PRD Quality Review — MBSC (serexpai) v1, post-reconciliation (2026-10-31)

## Overall verdict
Adequate, close to strong. The thesis is explicit and falsifiable ("v1 is therefore a rate-measurement experiment wrapped in a usable CLI"). The Baselines-only arm makes Gain attributable to Formulas, numeric bounds are now real (N + 11, 64 KiB, 4096 Formulas, 24 h), and scope and counter-metrics do honest work. The main risk is at the verdict's edges. The PRD's own research predicts a PCM-only or marginal win, and neither outcome has a stated meaning, so the "ambiguous outcome" the Vision rules out can still happen. A second, smaller risk is drift between the PRD and the spec in FR-8 and FR-14, plus pointers into the brief addendum that still describe rANS and an entropy screen v1 no longer has.

## Decision-readiness — adequate
Decisions are stated as decisions, and most say what was given up. Examples: "rANS is not used in v1" with the patent and format-bump cost of adopting it later (FR-8); "v1 has no entropy screen or other heuristic that can change which Candidate wins" (FR-6); "Do not trade rate-measurement fidelity … for speed" (SM-C3); "Both outcomes are acceptable; an unreproducible or ambiguous outcome is not" (§1). §8 now separates what was resolved from what is open, and each open item has an owner and a deadline. Open Question 1 is honestly labelled "Deferred, not blocking v1."

The gap is in what a decision-maker does with the result. SM-1 is binary ("any reduction", "at least two distinct Real input classes"), but the PRD's own evidence says the likely results sit at the boundary, and those cases have no decision rule.

### Findings
- **high** The most likely outcome has no stated meaning (§1, §7 SM-1, addendum "Inference to carry into benchmark expectations") — The addendum says text "will probably lose to order-0 or previous-byte baselines" and PCM "is therefore a likely win only for 8-bit PCM". Executables are not discussed. Under SM-1, a win on 8-bit PCM alone, with all three classes testable, is plainly *negative*. Yet the Vision's growth path (reversible preprocessing, byte-lane split) is exactly what a PCM-only win would argue for. A decision-maker reading "If it does not, the repository stays as a reproducible negative result" cannot tell whether a one-class win ends the project or justifies the deferred PCM experiments. The corpora companion also warns that LJ Speech "was sourced from 128 kbps MP3 (band-limited, may flatter smooth predictors)", which makes a PCM-only win more likely and less convincing. *Fix:* in §1 or §7, state per-class expectations and what a one-class win means: for example, "SM-1 negative; a single-class win (PCM) opens only the deferred PCM experiments in §5, not the open-source compressor path." Carry the MP3-source caveat next to SM-1.
- **medium** A marginal Gain has no interpretation rule (§3 Gain "No minimum margin; the report also states per-input wins and the margin so a marginal Gain can be interpreted"; FR-14 verdict line) — Reporting the margin is not a decision rule. A one-byte Gain over a 12 MiB class counts as positive, triggers "the project grows", and is exactly the ambiguous outcome §1 rejects. The corpora companion notes that evaluation inputs within a class are correlated ("one PCM speaker; the same packages built for two architectures"), so per-input wins overstate how independent the evidence is. *Fix:* keep SM-1's "any reduction" for the research answer, but add the threshold for the go decision. For example, a Gain that is a minimum share of the class's Total encoded size and wins on a majority of inputs, or "below X%, the verdict is reported as marginal and does not trigger §1's growth path." Mention the correlation caveat in FR-14.
- **medium** Patent review is gated on commercial release, not public release (§1 "Commercial use is planned, so licence and patent decisions must keep it open"; §8 OQ 5 "before the first commercial release"; §6.1 publication gate "mandatory before any public release") — The adaptive probability update chosen for v1 is the one OQ 5 says may read on live Qualcomm and Tencent patents. Publishing an open-source compressor before counsel's review exposes everyone who uses or redistributes it, and MIT OR Apache-2.0 grants no rights to third-party patents. The PRD may accept this risk, but it does not say so. *Fix:* either move OQ 5's deadline to "before the first public release", or record it as an accepted risk with a one-line reason.

## Substance over theater — strong
There is one real persona (Darian) plus a named, tagged reviewer (Mei) who drives the reproducibility and spec-sufficiency requirements. The commercial-use JTBD drives the licence and patent decisions; it is not decoration. The Vision is specific to this product and hedges its novelty ("apparently novel (a shallow literature search found …)"). There is no NFR boilerplate: every quality bound is a number inside an FR (N + 11, 64 KiB, 24 h, at most 4096 Formulas). The counter-metrics carry real content.

### Findings
- **low** The second Gain comparator is dominated by construction (§3 Gain "versus the Baselines-only arm and versus the best whole-input Baseline … `[ASSUMPTION: the stronger of the two possible comparators]`"; FR-7) — The Baselines-only arm offers a superset of every forced Baseline's Candidates under the same partition rule and exact-size selection, and Blocks are independent (the spec says the model resets at Block start). So the arm is never larger than the best whole-input Baseline, and the second comparator can never decide SM-1. The assumption it carries, and SM-1's sentence "A reduction that comes only from switching among Baselines does not count", are therefore redundant. *Fix:* keep the whole-input Baselines as report columns, but define Gain against the Baselines-only arm alone, and drop the assumption. Alternatively, state that the second comparator is a consistency check that must never bind.

## Strategic coherence — strong
The thesis is a single bet ("compact formulas, once their parameters and modelling overhead are paid for, leave residuals cheap enough to beat simple baselines"), and every scope choice follows from it. Retrieval, SIMD and preprocessing are deferred until "a rate result exists" (§6.2). Exhaustive search is kept as the reference that later indexes must be validated against (§4.2). The per-Block cost sidecar (FR-14) sets up that validation. The success metrics test the thesis, not activity: SM-1 compares against an arm with Formulas disabled, and SM-C1 and SM-C2 name the two ways the experiment could fool itself. The split between the measurement core and the publication gate (§6.1) resolves the earlier tension between experiment and publishable spec. This is a coherent problem-solving MVP.

### Findings
- **low** Executable tuning inputs do not represent the executables being evaluated (FR-15 "Canterbury and Calgary for tuning … two pinned Debian binaries for amd64 and arm64"; `corpora.md`) — Executable tuning uses Calgary `obj1` and `obj2` (VAX and Macintosh object code from the 1980s) and Canterbury `sum` (SPARC). Evaluation uses x86-64, arm64 and Win32 code. A Codebook tuned on unrepresentative executables could understate Formulas on that class, producing a negative result that partly reflects corpus choice. Given that SM-1 needs two classes, that matters. *Fix:* add a tuning executable for a modern ISA that passes the overlap check (for example, a different Debian package), or name the mismatch as a known bias next to SM-1.

## Done-ness clarity — adequate
Most FRs now have consequences a story writer can test directly: FR-1 (all 256 × 256 pairs), FR-5 (dynamic programming over a 256-byte grid), FR-6 (chosen size is at most every Candidate's on the same span; Raw Block wins unless strictly smaller), FR-9 (CRC-32; empty, sub-Block and one-Block inputs), FR-10 ("at most N + 11 bytes"), FR-13 ("at most 64 KiB, independent of N"). That is well above typical. The remaining gaps are one internal contradiction, one budget with no defined consequence if missed, and a few places where the PRD says less than the spec, or something different, on a testable point.

### Findings
- **medium** "Model signalling" contradicts FR-8 and hides the real overhead (FR-8 "nothing about it is transmitted" vs FR-8 "Per-Block model signalling cost is measured and reported" and FR-14 "header, Formula parameter, model signalling and payload bytes"; Glossary "Total encoded size … coder flush") — If nothing is transmitted, the model-signalling bucket is zero by construction, and a test of the measurement is meaningless. The spec measures something else instead: adaptation overhead (ideal adaptive code length minus zero-order entropy) and a separate 4-byte coder flush per Block. That flush is 32 bits per 256-byte Block. It is the fixed cost most likely to decide the thesis at short Blocks, and FR-14's breakdown does not list it. *Fix:* replace "model signalling" in FR-8 and FR-14 with "adaptation overhead" and "coder flush", and add the flush to the FR-14 breakdown.
- **medium** The time budget can't be checked and has no consequence if missed (FR-4 "so benchmark encoding fits the time budget of FR-14"; FR-14 "within 24 hours on one named host") — The host is not named. The fit is asserted as fact, but the spec calls the encode-cost figures "work-model estimates, not measurements". Roughly 65 MiB of corpus, including the 36 MB `enwik8.zip` control, at the spec's 1–5 ms per input byte per core comes to about 19–95 core-hours. That fits only with enough cores. The spec's fallback (shrink the corpus while keeping at least 3 evaluation inputs per class, then lower the Codebook cap and report the excess bytes) is the real trade-off, and the PRD omits it. *Fix:* tag the fit as `[ASSUMPTION]`, name the host (or the minimum core count), and add the fallback order to FR-14.
- **medium** Per-Block Candidate-cost retention is unbounded in the PRD (FR-14 "The report retains, per Block, the encoded cost of every evaluated Candidate") — Read literally, this means a sidecar of about 4100 Candidates × 3 lengths × 2 bytes per 256-byte offset. That is gigabytes for `enwik8.zip` alone. The spec limits the default sidecar to inputs of 4 MiB or less, with larger inputs opt-in. A story written from the PRD would build the wrong thing. *Fix:* carry the spec's limit into FR-14.
- **low** No tie-break rule (FR-6) — FR-5 requires "deterministic" results, and SM-1 attribution depends on how ties fall. The spec says "ties never favour a Formula over a Baseline; Whole-file raw wins ties and the empty input." The PRD is silent. *Fix:* add that sentence to FR-6.
- **low** Parameter ranges are given as "the review's examples" (FR-3 "the review's examples: Q in [8,24], counts 1 to 16") — These are now fixed in the spec, yet the PRD still frames them as examples, so the requirement has no stated range. *Fix:* state them as requirements.
- **low** No input size limit (FR-10 "at most N + 11 bytes"; FR-12) — The header's 5 length bytes cap N below 2³⁵, and the spec has `compress` refuse larger inputs. FR-12 has no consequence for an oversized input. *Fix:* add "rejects N ≥ 2³⁵ with a non-zero exit" to FR-12.
- **low** SM-4 doesn't cover reproduction on another machine (SM-4 "on a clean checkout yields identical Total encoded sizes" vs UJ-3 "reruns the benchmark on their machine, and gets the same sizes") — Nothing requires the encoder's selection to be identical across platforms or thread counts; FR-2 covers only the evaluator. *Fix:* "on any supported platform and thread count."

## Scope honesty — strong
§5 Non-Goals and §6.2 agree with each other, and each deferral gives a reason ("deferred until a rate result exists"). The new non-goals on integrity ("no per-Block checksums and no cryptographic integrity") and compatibility ("promises no Bitstream compatibility between 0.x versions") close real gaps a reader might otherwise fill in. Open items: 5 Open Questions (one explicitly deferred), 7 inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tags and 0 `[NOTE FOR PM]`, for 12 in total. Each open question has an owner and a phase deadline ("before spec freeze", "before the first benchmark run"). For a solo research build this density is fine, and none blocks starting implementation.

### Findings
- **low** No `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts despite live tensions — The one-class and marginal-Gain verdict cases, the Codebook cap against the 24 h budget, and the patent gate are all unresolved tensions with no callout. *Fix:* add callouts at SM-1 and FR-14, or answer them as the findings above suggest.
- **low** The 1 KiB overlap threshold is stated as settled but listed as open (FR-15 "A checker proves that no tuning input shares a run of 1 KiB or more" vs OQ 2 "and the 1 KiB overlap threshold"; `corpora.md` "K = 1 KiB, PROPOSED") — *Fix:* "a run of K bytes or more (1 KiB proposed, OQ 2)."
- **low** The spec document is called a "goal" in one place and mandatory in another (§5 "the spec document (FR-11) remains a goal" vs §6.1 "mandatory before any public release") — *Fix:* align §5 with the publication gate wording.

## Downstream usability — adequate
For a chain-top PRD, the structure holds up. FRs are numbered globally, every FR says which UJ it realizes, every SM names the FRs it validates, and the Glossary is used consistently for the core nouns (Block, Formula, Candidate, Baselines-only arm, Total encoded size). §0 says plainly that the spec is the canonical contract, which settles any conflict in the spec's favour. The weak spots are the pointers into the brief addendum that FR-6, FR-8 and FR-9 rely on: that document predates the range-coder and no-screen decisions.

### Findings
- **medium** Pointers into the brief addendum are stale (§0 "technical detail … that FR-6, FR-8 and FR-9 point to"; FR-6 "The formulas for rate and the raw comparison are in the brief addendum"; FR-8 "The brief addendum lists the causal-model rules (reverse-order decode, buffering of coding decisions)") — The brief addendum's rate section counts "rANS states" and recommends "zero-order entropy … only as a screen", which contradicts FR-6's "no entropy screen". FR-8's causal-model rules are rANS rules: v1's range coder encodes decisions in forward order, so reverse-order decoding and backward buffering don't apply. The FR-9 gap list still says "a transmitted or deterministic rANS model". Someone extracting FR-8 or FR-6 by following the pointer will design the wrong thing. *Fix:* point FR-6, FR-8 and FR-9 at the spec companions (`acceptance-criteria.md` CAP-4, CAP-6 and CAP-7), and keep the brief addendum only as rationale. Alternatively, mark which items there are superseded.
- **low** Glossary gaps and drift — "Work count" is used in FR-14 and §5 but not defined (the spec glossary defines it). The Baselines-only arm entry lists "Whole-file raw mode" among the "Candidate[s]", but a Candidate is defined per Block. FR-14 refers to the review's claim about "WAV" while the class is "8-bit PCM derived from LJ Speech" (FR-15). *Fix:* add Work count to the Glossary, describe Whole-file raw mode as an arm-level option rather than a Candidate, and say "PCM" in FR-14.

## Shape fit — strong
This is a solo research CLI with a public-release path. The PRD uses a capability-spec shape: three short UJs that exist to anchor FR traceability, no persona padding, and FRs written as requirements, with technical detail delegated to the spec ("points to it for technical detail", §0). The rigour is above hobby level, but the stakes justify it: the experiment's credibility and later publication both depend on reproducibility. It is not over-formalized. No findings.
